From a business question to a better next move.

Cross-industry marketing decision workflow
01Business questionDefine the decision+

Start with the change the business needs—not a channel. Is the priority awareness, qualified demand, adoption, retention, customer experience or portfolio focus? State the decision, audience and time horizon.

02Signal mapCollect what the market is saying+

Bring together customer questions, search behaviour, sales feedback, CRM patterns, competitor movement and channel performance. A signal is an observation, not yet a conclusion.

03Evidence checkTest what holds up+

Compare sources, recency and relevance. Look for patterns that appear in more than one place, identify contradictions and separate a useful lead from noise.

04Strategic choiceChoose the priority+

Select one audience problem and one next move. Make the reasoning visible, name the assumptions and clarify what will not be prioritised yet.

05Bounded testConnect message, channel and experience+

Define the message, route to market, customer experience, owner and review window. The test should be small enough to learn from and complete enough to reveal where friction sits.

06ReviewKeep, change or stop+

Read the outcome against the original decision. Keep what is supported, change what the evidence challenges and stop activity that no longer earns its place.

Illustrative operating model. The inputs, review cadence and decision criteria change with the industry, customer journey and evidence available.

Where does product interest become paid value?

Outside-in assessment of the audience, activation journey and path to paid value.

The question

How could the journey from listing photos to a finished video make the value of upgrading easier to understand?

The proposal

Map the path from upload to generation, preview, export and upgrade. Test the upgrade message at the point where the user can see the output, alongside clearer measurement of each step.

The sequence

Where users leave the journey, which customers pay and return, and which product changes are feasible.
